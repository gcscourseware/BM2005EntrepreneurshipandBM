Section 01
Executive Summary
The executive summary is the most critical section — often the only part investors read first. It encapsulates your entire business vision in one compelling overview.
  • Write it last, despite appearing first
  • State your mission, product, and market in 2 sentences
  • Highlight the problem, solution, and traction
  • Include key financial highlights and funding ask
  • Keep it to 1–2 pages maximum
Critical — Read First by Investors

What is the Business Model Canvas?

Developed by Alexander Osterwalder, the BMC is a strategic management template for developing new or documenting existing business models. It offers a visual chart with elements describing a firm's value proposition, infrastructure, customers, and finances.

Replaces 40-page business plans
Enables rapid iteration and pivoting
Aligns teams around a shared model
Supports hypothesis-driven validation
Visual and collaborative by design
Works for any industry or scale

Step 1 of 7
Define Customer Segments
Everything starts with the customer. Use Post-it notes to brainstorm every potential customer type. Then apply the Value Proposition Canvas to understand their jobs-to-be-done, pain points, and desired gains before writing anything else.
  • Write each segment on a separate sticky note
  • Prioritise segments by size and willingness to pay
  • Interview at least 5 people per segment
  • Validate assumptions with real market data
Start with your most promising customer segment — don't try to serve everyone at once.
Topic 02 — Validation Framework

Using the BMC to Validate Ideas

1
Phase 1 — Hypotheses

Identify Assumptions

Each block of the BMC represents a hypothesis about your business. The riskiest assumptions must be tested first before you invest resources building the product.

  • List every assumption in each BMC block
  • Rank by risk: desirability, feasibility, viability
  • Prioritise the 3 most critical assumptions
  • Frame each as a testable hypothesis
2
Phase 2 — Experiments

Design & Run Tests

For each hypothesis, design the smallest, cheapest experiment that could prove or disprove it. Customer interviews, landing pages, and concierge MVPs are ideal tools.

  • Define clear success metrics upfront
  • Customer discovery interviews (15–20 min each)
  • Smoke test landing pages with ad traffic
  • Wizard of Oz or concierge MVP tests
3
Phase 3 — Decisions

Pivot or Persevere

Use evidence gathered to update your BMC. The canvas is a living document — iterate rapidly based on what you learn. Failed hypotheses are insights, not failures.

  • Update the BMC after every experiment
  • Document learnings in a test card log
  • Pivot the block with weak evidence
  • Persevere when multiple signals align
Topic 03 — Section A

Crafting an Elevator Pitch

The Pitch Formula

For [Target Customer]
+
Who [Has Problem]
Our [Product/Service]
+
Provides [Key Benefit]
Unlike [Main Alternative], We [Key Differentiator]
60s
The Golden Rule

An elevator pitch must land in 30–90 seconds — the duration of a real elevator ride. Memorise it. Practise it until it sounds completely natural.

Example: "For busy urban professionals who struggle to eat healthily, NutriBox delivers personalised weekly meal kits in under 20 minutes — unlike generic meal services, every box is tailored by a registered dietitian to your exact health goals."
🎯
Lead with the Problem

Investors fund solutions to real problems. Open with the pain point before describing your product.

💬
Use Plain Language

Avoid jargon and acronyms. If a 12-year-old can't understand it, rewrite it until they can.

📊
Include One Wow Stat

A single compelling data point (market size, growth rate, or traction metric) makes you memorable.

🔄
End with a Hook

Close with a question or clear ask: "We're raising $500K — would you have 30 minutes next week?"

🎭
Practise Relentlessly

Record yourself. Pitch to strangers. Attend pitch events. Refinement comes from repetition, not perfection.

Topic 03 — Section B

Presenting to Investors & Stakeholders

What Investors Look For

  • Exceptional founding team with relevant domain expertise
  • Large, growing addressable market (TAM/SAM/SOM)
  • Clear, defensible value proposition and moat
  • Early traction: revenue, users, or letters of intent
  • Realistic financial model with a path to profitability
  • A compelling and coherent use-of-funds breakdown

The 10-Slide Pitch Deck

1

2

3

4

5

7

8

9
Financials — 3-year projections
10
The Ask — Amount & use of funds

Presentation Best Practices

  • Send the deck before the meeting so investors arrive informed
  • Use visuals over text — one key message per slide
  • Open strong — your first 60 seconds set the entire tone
  • Tell a narrative arc, not a feature list
  • Know your numbers cold — every projection and metric
  • Leave 30% of time for Q&A and genuine dialogue

Room & Body Language

  • Make deliberate eye contact with each person in the room
  • Stand and own the space — confidence is contagious
  • Control your pacing — pause after key statements
  • Bring printed one-pagers as a leave-behind
  • Follow up within 24 hours with a recap email
  • Track investor interactions in a simple CRM
Topic 03 — Section C

Handling Feedback & Objections

"Your market is too small."
▾
Acknowledge the concern, then reframe with a bottoms-up market analysis. Show your beachhead strategy — start small, then expand. Reference adjacent markets you can enter as you scale. Present a 5-year land-and-expand roadmap.
"You have too much competition."
▾
Competition validates the market exists. Show a competitive positioning matrix highlighting your unique differentiators. Explain your defensible moat: network effects, switching costs, proprietary data, or brand loyalty that incumbents cannot replicate easily.
"Your projections seem optimistic."
▾
Present three scenarios: base, bull, and bear cases. Anchor your model to your current conversion rates and CAC, then show the assumptions behind each growth driver. Invite scrutiny — it shows confidence and a deep understanding of your unit economics.
"I don't understand the technology."
▾
Simplify immediately using an analogy they know. "Think of us as Uber, but for X." Bring a live demo or short video. Offer to connect the investor with a technical advisor for a deeper dive. Never let complexity be a reason to lose the conversation.
"Your team lacks experience."
▾
Highlight complementary skills across the founding team. Show what you've already achieved despite being early-stage. Name experienced advisors or board members. Demonstrate a hiring plan that fills identified gaps as capital is deployed.
"The valuation is too high."
▾
Reference comparable valuations in your sector using recent funding rounds. Explain the key milestones you'll hit with this funding that will justify the next round's valuation. Show the investor the potential multiple on their investment at exit.

The AREA Framework

A
Acknowledge

Validate the concern without becoming defensive. "That's a fair point, thank you."

R
Reframe

Shift perspective with data or a different lens. "Here's another way to look at it…"

E
Evidence

Support your reframe with facts, metrics, or examples. "Our data shows…"

A
Advance

Move the conversation forward. "Does that address your concern, or shall we go deeper?"

"Every objection is a gift — it reveals exactly what you need to address to earn their trust and capital."
