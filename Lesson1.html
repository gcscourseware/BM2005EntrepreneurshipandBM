Definition & Characteristics

"Entrepreneurship is the process of designing, launching, and running a new business — typically a start-up — offering a product, process, or service. It is the willingness to take on risk in pursuit of opportunity."
— Schumpeter's Theory of Innovation
🎯
Risk Tolerance

Comfortable navigating uncertainty and calculated financial/personal risk.

💡

Creating new products, services, or processes that disrupt existing markets.

🔥

Intrinsic motivation and relentless pursuit of a vision despite obstacles.

🔄
Adaptability

Pivoting quickly when conditions change or initial plans don't materialise.

👥
Leadership

Inspiring teams, attracting talent, and building organisational culture.

📈
Value Creation

Generating economic, social, or environmental value for stakeholders.

Topic 02 — Section B

Incubators, Accelerators & Mentors

1–5 Years
🏠

Incubators

Nurture early-stage ventures with long-term support. Provide shared workspace, seed funding, mentorship, and business development resources in a low-pressure environment.

  • Subsidised office space & facilities
  • Access to seed capital & grants
  • Expert mentorship & coaching
  • Legal, HR & accounting support
  • Examples: Y Combinator, Techstars
3–6 Months
⚡

Accelerators

Intensive fixed-term programmes that rapidly scale start-ups. Cohort-based with structured curriculum, mentorship, and culminate in Demo Day for investor pitches.

  • Structured curriculum & milestones
  • Equity investment in exchange
  • Demo Day investor exposure
  • Peer cohort network & alumni
  • Examples: 500 Global, Plug and Play
Ongoing
🎓

Mentors

Experienced entrepreneurs and industry experts who provide personalised guidance, open doors through networks, and share hard-won wisdom to help founders avoid common pitfalls.

  • 1:1 strategic & tactical advice
  • Industry-specific domain expertise
  • Warm introductions to investors
  • Emotional support & accountability
  • Peer advisory boards & circles

Topic 03 — Section A

Market Research & Analysis

1
Define Research Objectives

Clarify what you need to know — market size, customer pain points, competitor landscape, or pricing sensitivity.

2
Identify Target Segments

Use demographics, psychographics, and behavioural data to pinpoint who your customer actually is.

3
Collect Primary & Secondary Data

Interviews, surveys, and observation for primary. Industry reports, census data, and competitor analysis for secondary.

4
Analyse & Synthesise

Look for patterns, contradictions, and surprising insights. Quantify where possible; qualify where needed.

5
Apply Insights to Strategy

Translate findings into product decisions, positioning, pricing, and go-to-market strategy with confidence.

Research Methods

Surveys
Interviews
Observation
Analytics

Quantitative Surveys

Structured questionnaires distributed to large samples. Ideal for measuring attitudes, frequency, and preferences at scale.

  • Use Google Forms or Typeform for rapid deployment
  • Keep surveys under 10 questions for completion rates
  • Use Likert scales for nuanced attitude measurement
  • Aim for 100+ responses for statistical significance

Customer Interviews

Deep 1:1 conversations that uncover motivations, workarounds, and unspoken needs that surveys miss entirely.

  • Use open-ended "why" and "how" questions
  • Follow the Jobs-to-be-Done framework
  • Record and transcribe with permission
  • Aim for 15–20 interviews to reach saturation

Ethnographic Observation

Watching real users in their natural context reveals behaviour that differs from what people say they do in surveys.

  • Shadow customers during purchase journeys
  • Note workarounds and frustrations silently
  • Use screen recording tools for digital products
  • Document non-verbal cues and environment

Data & Analytics

Leverage existing digital data trails to understand market behaviour, search trends, and competitive positioning.

  • Google Trends for demand validation
  • SEMrush / Ahrefs for keyword and SEO analysis
  • SimilarWeb for competitor traffic insights
  • Social listening via Brandwatch or Sprout Social
Topic 03 — Section B

Identifying Gaps & Opportunities

🔍

Underserved Segments

Identify groups whose needs are partially or poorly met by existing solutions. These customers are primed for a better alternative.

Senior tech usersRural healthcare access
💸

Price Gap Arbitrage

Find markets where premium products exist but affordable alternatives don't — or vice versa. Disruption often lives in the middle.

Budget legal servicesPremium pet food
🌍

Geographic White Spaces

Successful models in one geography often haven't reached others. Import proven solutions to untapped markets with adaptation.

Fintech in SEAEdTech in Africa

2

3

Structured ideation via 7 thinking lenses

4

Build-measure-learn validation loops

5
Pretotype Testing

Test the idea before building the product

Brainstorming

A group creativity technique where participants generate as many ideas as possible without immediate judgement. Quantity precedes quality — wild ideas often spark practical innovations.

  • Set a clear problem statement before you begin
  • No criticism during the generation phase
  • Build on others' ideas using "yes, and…"
  • Use mind-mapping to cluster related ideas
Validation Check

After brainstorming, apply a feasibility filter: Is it desirable? Is it technically feasible? Is it financially viable? Only ideas passing all three proceed.
